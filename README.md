Nexus Financial Services: Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) Assessment

This project examines governance, risk management, and compliance challenges at Nexus Financial Services, a growing financial services company in Lagos, Nigeria. It identifies gaps in policy management, risk assessment, accountability, and regulatory tracking, then recommends practical steps to establish a more structured GRC programme.

Project Overview

Nexus Financial Services has experienced business growth without establishing a formal framework to guide its decisions, manage risks, and monitor compliance obligations.

The case study identifies three primary issues:

Governance: No clear process for approving and enforcing company policies.

Risk Management: No standard process for assessing risks before making business decisions.

Compliance: No designated responsibility for tracking regulatory requirements and deadlines.

The objectives of this project were to:

Explain Governance, Risk, and Compliance in simple terms.

Identify organizational issues affecting Nexus Financial Services.

Categorize each issue under the appropriate GRC pillar.

Recommend an action to improve the company's GRC programme.

Develop a practical approach to accountability, risk assessment, policy management, and compliance monitoring.

The assessment demonstrates how a structured GRC programme can help an organization make informed decisions, manage uncertainty, and meet its obligations as it grows.

Configuration Steps

Review the business environment. Examined Nexus Financial Services' growth, informal decision-making practices, and lack of a formal GRC framework.

Explain the three GRC pillars. Defined Governance as organizational direction and accountability, Risk Management as identifying and treating uncertainty, and Compliance as meeting applicable obligations.

Identify governance issues. Identified the lack of clear processes for policy approval, enforcement, and accountability.

Identify risk management issues. Highlighted the absence of a standard process for assessing risks before business decisions are made.

Identify compliance issues. Identified the lack of assigned responsibility for tracking regulatory requirements and deadlines.

Categorize the issues. Mapped policy approval and accountability to Governance, risk assessment to Risk Management, and regulatory tracking to Compliance.

Recommend a GRC programme. Proposed leadership sponsorship, defined responsibilities, a policy management process, a risk register, and a compliance obligations register.

Develop an implementation plan. Recommended a phased approach to assigning owners, documenting obligations, assessing risks, and reporting progress to leadership.

Document the findings. Prepared a professional assessment summarizing the issues, recommendations, expected outcomes, and limitations of the available information.

Results and Findings

The assessment identified gaps across all three GRC pillars.

Governance Findings

Policy approval and enforcement processes are unclear.

Decision-making responsibilities and accountability need to be defined.

Leadership should establish oversight and ensure that policies are communicated and followed.

Risk Management Findings

Nexus lacks a standard process for assessing risks before business decisions.

A documented risk register would improve visibility into potential threats, business impacts, and treatment actions.

Risk owners should be assigned to ensure that identified risks are monitored and addressed.

Compliance Findings

Responsibility for tracking regulatory requirements and deadlines is unclear.

A compliance obligations register would help identify applicable requirements, owners, review dates, and evidence.

Overdue compliance actions should be escalated to the appropriate management level.

Overall Recommendation

Establish a leadership-sponsored GRC programme with defined responsibilities and repeatable processes.

The programme should include:

An approved GRC charter and clear accountability.

A structured policy management process.

A risk assessment methodology and maintained risk register.

A compliance obligations register with assigned owners and deadlines.

Regular reporting, remediation tracking, and management review.

A dedicated GRC team or cross-functional working group can coordinate these activities. However, establishing a team alone will not resolve the issues. Leadership and business owners must remain accountable for their respective decisions, risks, policies, and compliance obligations.

The case study does not identify specific regulatory violations, confirmed fines, or actual compliance incidents. Further assessment would be required to establish these facts.
